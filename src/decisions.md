# How decisions are made

We take decisions close to the work, write them down where the work is, and escalate along a fixed path when agreement fails.

## Who decides what

| Decision | Taken by | Recorded in |
|---|---|---|
| Technical choices inside a task | The task team | The pull request |
| Choices that constrain the design: a parameter, an interface, a component, a licence | The task team proposes, the department lead or a member Qualified in the area reviews, and Direction accepts | A Design Decision Record |
| Starting to build a machine | Direction, with the Tutor's agreement; for a machine built for users, after their go | The team's plan |
| Safety rules and authorisations | The Tutor | The safety documentation |
| Budget, purchases, sponsors and external relations | Direction | Direction's records |
| Admission of members and selection of department leads | Direction | Direction's records |
| The next season's Coordinator and Co-Lead | The Tutor | Direction's records |

## Reversible and irreversible decisions

A decision that can be undone cheaply is taken by whoever is doing the work and noted in the pull request. A decision that is costly to undo needs a Design Decision Record before work proceeds: purchases, frozen interfaces, changes to published parameters and anything that affects safety.

## Consent by default

A proposal posted where the work is discussed stands if nobody objects within three working days during term. An objection states a reason and, where possible, an alternative.

## Escalation

When a task team cannot agree, its convener takes the question to the Coordinator. Questions of safety or of institutional weight go to the Tutor, whose decision is final.

## The plan and the record

Direction keeps a plan for the coming seasons and reviews it every September, including the decision on whether to build ALOHA-1. This handbook is revised at the same time.

A decision exists once it is written in a pull request or a Design Decision Record. Agreements reached in a meeting or in chat are written down the same day by whoever proposed them.
