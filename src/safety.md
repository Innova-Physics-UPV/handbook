# Safety

We work with high voltage, evacuated vessels and, from the first beam onward, X-rays. We control these hazards first by design and then by procedure.

## Principles

- **Fail-safe design.** Equipment is designed so that any single failure brings it to a safe state. We aim for a residual risk comparable to that of a standard teaching laboratory.
- **Authority.** The Tutor holds final authority on safety. Equipment involving high voltage, vacuum or the beam is operated only with the Tutor's authorisation.
- **Qualification.** Members operate equipment only in the areas in which they are Qualified. Candidates do not work on laboratory equipment.
- **Two people.** Work on energised or evacuated equipment requires at least two people present, one of them Qualified in the area.
- **Interlocks.** Interlocks and emergency stops are designed, reviewed and tested like any other subsystem.
- **Licences.** Any licence or authorisation the law requires is held by university staff, never by a student.
- **Records.** Every operation of laboratory equipment is entered in the laboratory logbook.

## Radiation

Electrons stopping in matter produce X-rays. Shielding is part of every machine's design. The university's radiation protection service measures the dose rate of each ALOHA machine before its first beam, and again before any handover.

## Procedures

The safety rules in force, the list of authorised operators and the procedure for each system are kept in the team's internal documentation.
